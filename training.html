Web editor quick reference

For faculty and staff who update their department's pages: the five pieces you'll use most, the mistakes the web team fixes most often, and what to check before you publish.

Web designers: hand this out at the end of a training session. Choose Print in your browser and it fits on one sheet of letter or A4 paper.

The five pieces you'll use most

Copy the HTML, then change only the words between the tags. The style guide shows how each one looks.

  • Headings

    Break the page into sections people can scan. The page title is the only heading 1, sections get a heading 2, and their parts a heading 3. Choose by the outline, not the size, and never skip a level.

    <h2>What you'll study</h2>
<h3>First year</h3>
<p>Every biology student starts with…</p>

  • Links

    The link's words say where it goes, so they make sense on their own. For a list of pages, use a link list.

    <p>Read the <a href="apply.html">steps to apply</a>.</p>
<ul class="link-list">
  <li><a href="apply.html">How to apply</a>
    <p>Four steps and the deadlines.</p></li>
</ul>

  • Buttons

    For the main thing you want visitors to do: one solid button per page, quiet ones for the rest. Say what happens: "Request a meeting," not "Submit."

    <a class="button" href="advising.html">Request a meeting</a>
<a class="button button--quiet" href="events.html">All events</a>

  • Tables

    For things people compare across rows, like courses, never for layout. Keep the caption and column headers. Each caption needs its own id, repeated in the wrapper's aria-labelledby.

    <section class="table-wrap" tabindex="0" aria-labelledby="core-caption">
  <table>
    <caption id="core-caption">Core courses</caption>
    <thead><tr><th scope="col">Course</th><th scope="col">Title</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody><tr><td>BIO 230</td><td>Genetics</td></tr></tbody>
  </table>
</section>

  • Callout

    A shaded box for the one next step you want visitors to take. Use one per page: if everything stands out, nothing does.

    <section class="callout" aria-labelledby="advisor">
  <h2 id="advisor">Talk with an advisor</h2>
  <p>Get a sample four-year plan.</p>
  <p><a class="button" href="advising.html">Request a meeting</a></p>
</section>

Mistakes to avoid

Common mistakes, and what to do instead
Instead ofDo this
"Click here" or "Read more" linksName the page in the link: "See the biology courses."
Pasting from Word or Google DocsPaste as plain text: Ctrl+Shift+V, or Cmd+Shift+Option+V on a Mac.
Words in a picture, like a flyerType the words on the page, where screen readers and search can find them.
A PDF in place of a pagePut it on the page. Link a PDF only for a form to print: "Housing form (PDF)."
A photo straight from your phoneSend it to the web team to resize, with alt text that says what it adds.
Color alone to mark somethingSay it in words too: "Required" or "Due October 1."
"This fall" or "next Friday"Write the date. Pages stay up long after you write them.

Before you publish

  • Read the page on your phone.
  • Press Tab to move through it: you reach every link and button, and can see where you are.
  • Links go where they say.
  • Photos have alt text.
  • Names, dates and rooms are right.
