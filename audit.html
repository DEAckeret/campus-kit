Content audit checklist

Once a semester, each department goes through its web pages: what's wrong, what nobody needs any more, and who fixes what by when. This page is the checklist. The spreadsheet is where you write down what you find.

Download the spreadsheet (Excel, 7 KB) Column headings only (CSV)

The spreadsheet opens in Excel, Google Sheets, Numbers and LibreOffice. It has a blank Pages tab with drop-down lists, an Example tab with a finished audit, and a How to use tab.

How the audit works

  1. List your department's pages

    One row per page, with its title, address and owner. Ask the web team for the list: they can send it with last semester's visits filled in, so you don't miss a page nobody remembers.

  2. Read each page with the checklist

    Go through the five checks below, on your phone as well as a computer. In the spreadsheet, mark each check "Yes" or "Needs work." Most pages take five to ten minutes.

  3. Decide what happens to each page

    Keep it, update it, combine it with another page, or remove it. The table under the checklist says when to choose which.

  4. Say who fixes what, by when

    For anything but "Keep," write what to change clearly enough that someone else could do it, name one person or office, and give a date.

  5. Send the spreadsheet to the web team

    They combine and remove pages and send old addresses to the right new ones, so links from other sites still work. You fill in "Done on" as each fix goes up.

The checklist

A page passes a check only if every line under it is true. If one isn't, mark the check "Needs work" and write what's wrong under "What to change."

Correct

  • Names, job titles, phone numbers and email addresses are right, and the people still work here.
  • Dates, times, rooms, costs and deadlines are this year's.
  • Course numbers and program requirements match the current catalog.
  • Nothing says "this fall," "next year," "new" or "coming soon" about something that's already happened.

Still needed

  • Someone still comes to this page to do something: apply, register, find a person or a form.
  • No other page on the site says the same thing. If two do, they'll stop matching.
  • It isn't about an event, a course or a program that has ended.

Clear

  • The title says what the page is, and the description under it in search results says what's on it.
  • Headings break the page into parts you can scan, in order, with one main heading.
  • It's written in plain words for the person reading it, not in office shorthand.
  • The next step, such as applying, booking or emailing someone, is easy to find.

Accessible

  • Photos and charts have alt text that says what they add.
  • Link words say where each link goes. No "click here" or "read more."
  • Tables have column headers and a caption, and aren't used for layout.
  • Videos have captions. Nothing is a scanned PDF or a picture of words.
  • Color isn't the only way something is marked, such as a deadline in red.

Works

  • Every link and button goes where it says. Links to other sites still work.
  • Forms send, and the person who gets them still reads that inbox.
  • On a phone, nothing is cut off and you don't have to scroll sideways.

Deciding what happens to a page

The four decisions, and when to choose each one
DecisionChoose it whenWhat happens next
KeepAll five checks are "Yes."Change "Last reviewed" to today. Nothing else.
UpdatePeople still need the page, but something on it is wrong or hard to use.The person named fixes it by the due date. Check it again when it's done.
CombineTwo pages cover the same thing, or a page is too thin to stand on its own.The web team moves what's worth keeping onto one page and sends the other address there.
RemoveNobody needs it any more: an event that's over, a course or program that has ended, a page that only repeats another.The web team takes it down and sends its address to the closest page, so old links don't end at "page not found."

Few visits isn't a reason on its own to remove a page. Some pages are needed by only a few people, like the accessibility statement or a policy page, and still have to be there.

When to audit

  • Fall semester: send the spreadsheet by October 15, before college fairs and fall visit days.
  • Spring semester: by March 15, before admitted-student visits and course registration.

Add a page you've just made to your list right away, so it's in the next audit.
