Six years of river data, collected one first-year class at a time

By Alex Ortega, Marketing and Communications. Published October 2, 2026.

Every fall since 2020, Sleeping Giant biology students have waded into Rattlesnake Creek in their second week of classes. The result is one of the longest student-collected water records in the region.

The assignment sounds simple: measure temperature, dissolved oxygen and insect life at four spots along the creek. But because every class uses the same sites and the same methods, the numbers add up. This year's students can compare their samples against more than 2,000 earlier readings.

“The first year it was just a lab exercise,” said Dr. Priya Raman, who designed the project. “By year three, students were noticing trends I hadn't seen yet.”

From class project to state data

Last spring, the state's water quality office asked to include the college’s readings in its regional report. Two seniors presented the data at the agency's annual meeting, and one now works there as a seasonal technician.

Join the project

The creek study is part of BIO 121, open to any first-year student. Community members can see the full data set at the research poster session on October 28.
